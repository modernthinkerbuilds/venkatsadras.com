Rahat

Miya is the one I talk to. Rahat is everything underneath: the specialist agents, the checks between an answer and me, the harness Bourdain's answers run through, and a record of every decision. It runs around the clock on a Mac mini at home.

  • 6 agents
  • 3 chat bots
  • 4,209 tests
  • 86 real asks in the eval sets
  • 7,408 decisions logged

Agents and tests counted in the repo on Oct 17, 2026; the eval sets (Oct 17) and the decision ledger (Oct 14) are private, so those are my counts.

Personal scale, real problems

Rahat is not enterprise software. But a fluent agent that's confidently wrong, agents that disagree, a decision you can't prove and an evaluator you can't trust are the problems a company hits the day it runs a fleet. The same parts have company names.

How an agent joins

Bourdain is the first agent to start on its own chat bot, with its own tests, where its failures are easy to see and can't break anything else. It has been there since Sep 20 and on the harness since Oct 4; when it passes, it's wired in behind Miya, and Miya stays the one place I ask. Genie went the other way round, behind Miya on Aug 6 and then its own bot for the household on Aug 10. Bourdain's route is the route from here on.

Orchestration is the part I'm least done with. Today sixteen rules in code answer the asks they recognize, most without a model, and Miya's model handles the rest. Routing each ask by what every agent declares it can do is planned, not built.
