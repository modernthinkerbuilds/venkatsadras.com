The day job

At Google Cloud, I take Gemini Enterprise and its AI agents into banks, healthcare and government, safely. I define what the product has to be before those customers can run it, from the models it serves to the agents built on it.

In practice that has meant: what the platform had to become for US defense and national security, and for export-controlled aerospace; the access model that European banks' regulators and Korea's financial sector required, which became the pattern for sovereign markets; pre-authorized foundations for stateful agents that every product built on them inherits; a control that kept regulated projects safe by default while a flagship launch shipped on time for everyone else; and a portfolio baseline agreed across seven product directors. The part I like most is the week a market that couldn't run AI can, and knowing which spec made the difference.

At home

I built Rahat, my own open-source AI agent platform, and run seven agents on it: Miya, the one I talk to, and its team. They run in my family's life every day, which means I feel every failure the same day.

I care about agents you'd point at your own life: ones that remember, coordinate, check themselves, and know when not to act. Most of what goes wrong with agents isn't in the model. It's in the layer underneath, and that's the layer I like to build.

Off the keyboard

  • Dad of two. Much of Rahat was built on paternity leave, with our youngest supervising most of it.
  • CrossFit. My reset. It's also why two agents on the team exist: Fraser and Huberman.
  • Learning the guitar. Slowly. A good reminder of what it's like to be a beginner.
  • Food and travel. Which is how a food agent ended up on a family trip to New York and Boston, before it was ready. That's the series.

Underneath all of it: as much time with family as I can get, having fun and living life. The agents are there to give time back, not take it.
